Yes, the architecture is **contract-type agnostic by design** — the chunker splits on structural markers (numbered headings, UPPERCASE titles), not NDA-specific keywords. Any well-formatted legal PDF will chunk correctly.

But you're right to be cautious. The assessment spec had **3 contract pairs**:

**Pair 1: NDA v1 → v2** ✅ Tested

**Pair 2: SPA (Share Purchase Agreement) v1 → v2** — the hard one
- Tests the **clause SPLIT** detection: v1 has `8. Indemnification` as one clause, v2 splits it into `8. Direct` + `8A. Third Party`
- Your comparator will currently mark clause 8 as REMOVED + two new ADDED — wrong
- This is the semantic matching edge case — L2 TF-IDF should catch that `8A` content is similar to old `8`, but the SPLIT pattern itself isn't explicitly handled

**Pair 3: Employment Agreement v1 → v2** — should work fine
- Standard numbered clauses, clean structure
- C-011 will fire on the 18-month non-compete (once Fix 3 is in)
- C-014 will fire on the 30-day termination notice

**For the "surprise-ready" requirement** (unknown contract types), the main risk is:

- PDFs with **no numbered clauses** — e.g. a contract written as pure prose paragraphs. Your chunker will return 1 chunk (the graceful degradation fallback). That's acceptable but worth noting in architecture.md.
- PDFs with **Roman numerals** (`Article I`, `Article II`) — not currently in your boundary patterns. Add `re.compile(r'^[ \t]*(Article\s+[IVXLC]+)\.?\s+([A-Z][^\n]{2,100})$', re.MULTILINE)` to `_BOUNDARY_PATTERNS`.
- **Scanned PDFs** (image-only) — OCR fallback handles this, but quality depends on scan resolution.

The SPA clause-split case is the one gap worth explicitly acknowledging in `architecture.md` — "1:1 matching only; clause splits detected as REMOVED+ADDED rather than SPLIT" is an honest limitation that shows you understand the problem.
